# Country Market Scan Methodology

Use this methodology when evaluating a country-level distributed-energy,
microgrid, solar-plus-storage, or related infrastructure opportunity.

The final objective is not simply to prove that demand exists.
The objective is to determine whether the market deserves the next unit of
research, customer discovery, supplier engagement, or business-development effort.

## 1. Demand and pain

Establish:

- electricity access;
- reliability and outage conditions;
- diesel or backup-generation dependence;
- underserved customer segments;
- load growth or electrification pressure.

Separate lack of electricity from unreliable electricity.

## 2. Customer and buyer structure

Identify actual economic actors:

- C&I customers;
- agricultural and productive-use customers;
- telecom or tower operators;
- communities;
- utilities or DisCos;
- developers;
- IPPs;
- EPCs;
- public agencies.

Ask:

- Who signs the contract?
- Who pays?
- Who owns the asset?
- Who operates it?

## 3. Project economics and commercial model

Look for evidence of:

- tariffs;
- diesel-displacement economics;
- PPAs;
- energy-as-a-service;
- lease or financed ownership;
- subsidy structures;
- results-based financing;
- project size and contract duration.

Do not describe a market as commercially attractive without some evidence about
how projects are paid for.

## 4. Regulation

Research:

- mini-grid regulation;
- generation or distribution licensing;
- registration thresholds;
- capacity limits;
- tariff rules;
- interconnection;
- grid-arrival rules;
- import or local-content requirements where relevant.

Distinguish regulation currently in force from announced reforms.

## 5. Capital and financing

Identify:

- government programs;
- World Bank / IFC;
- AfDB;
- development-finance institutions;
- commercial banks;
- developer equity;
- grants;
- guarantees;
- results-based finance.

Distinguish committed, approved, targeted, and actually deployed capital.

## 6. Market execution evidence

Prefer evidence that projects actually happen:

- commissioned projects;
- signed PPAs;
- active developers;
- procurement awards;
- operating mini-grids;
- deployed BESS;
- customer contracts.

Execution evidence is generally more decision-useful than broad market forecasts.

## 7. Competition and ecosystem

Identify:

- developers;
- EPCs;
- equipment suppliers;
- local integrators;
- financiers;
- technology partners;
- O&M providers.

Do not assume that strong demand implies easy market entry.

## 8. Supply-chain fit

For Frontier Global Lab, assess where China-linked supply chains may plausibly add value:

- PV modules;
- BESS;
- PCS / inverter;
- switchgear;
- transformers;
- control panels;
- EMS / microgrid controllers;
- monitoring;
- remote or AI-assisted O&M.

Do not conclude that China supply is competitive merely because Chinese imports exist.

Look for evidence of:

- procurement patterns;
- specifications;
- local integration requirements;
- certification;
- pricing pressure;
- maintenance capability;
- localization expectations.

## 9. Decision synthesis

Separate the final synthesis into:

FACT:
What is directly evidenced.

INFERENCE:
What the combined facts suggest.

ASSUMPTION:
What still requires customer interviews, supplier quotes, field validation, or further research.

UNKNOWN:
What could not be established.

A provisional market verdict may be:

- PRIORITIZE
- WATCH
- DEPRIORITIZE

The verdict is always an INFERENCE, never a FACT.

Explain what evidence could cause the verdict to change.
